# Would I Play This? — A Fun Review of FABLEWOOD

> Provenance: Fable orchestrator, inline, 2026-07-22 (~04:30), on the author's brief: *"imagine you
> were an user. imagine you were playing this game. would you wanna play it? is it good? how
> could it be more fun?"* Grounded in: full design context of every shipped system, plus a
> deliberately naive playtest run tonight by a fresh-eyes agent that knew nothing (real dev
> server, real judge, real typed answers; transcript + ~95 screenshots at the session
> scratchpad under `naive-player/`, verdict in `VERDICT.md`). Where I cite "the naive player,"
> that's a real quote from that run, not a hypothetical.

## 1. Would I want to play it?

Yes — and I can name the exact moment that would hook me, because it hooked the naive player
tonight without anyone pointing at it: they bombed a real question, got handed the answer in
the Clerk's dry voice, and a few minutes later hit the same question and nailed it. Their
words: *"That's the whole promise of a 'knowledge-retention RPG' working exactly as intended,
felt from the inside."* The fail → teach → redeem loop is the product's soul, and it already
works. A game that makes me *feel* my own memory improving is offering something no other game
I know offers. I would come back for that feeling.

The honest qualifier: I'd come back **for a subject I actually care about retaining.** The
game's fun is a multiplier on the content's value to you — it's a lifetime-knowledge engine
wearing a very charming game, not a game you'd play with placeholder trivia. For its actual
audience (the author, storing a life's knowledge) that's not a weakness; it's the design working.

## 2. Is it good?

**What's genuinely working:**

- **"Your knowledge is your moveset"** — the Arts mapping question kinds to combat abilities
  is the cleverest structural idea in the game, and the naive player independently ranked it
  delight #1. It makes studying feel like building a character.
- **The writing voice is the single strongest asset.** The playtest verdict quoted failure
  lines verbatim as *delights* — "The Ledger records a far more spectacular ascent than such
  modest doubling" for a wrong answer about DEC's 5,000x. When your fail states are the lines
  players screenshot, failure has stopped being punishment and become content. Protect this
  voice above almost everything.
- **The world-as-curriculum joke lands** — "The Anti-Dilution Wraiths," "The Pay-to-Play
  Gauntlet." Real mechanics as dungeon monsters reads as wit, not gimmick.
- **Stakes without cruelty.** Teeth-but-fair held up in blind play: HP loss and retreat felt
  tense, never spiteful; the boss's shield-gate reads as "prove you understand" rather than
  RNG. Both live boss kills this week cost exactly 4/16 HP — dangerous but fair, measured.
- **The cozy register is real** — hamlet, music, lantern palette. The naive player's cold-open
  screenshot caption was simple delight.

**Where the fun leaks (verdicts from tonight, fixes already in flight marked ⚙):**

1. ⚙ **Fairness-trust cracks.** A 13s first "the Clerk deliberates…" with a bare ellipsis
   (long enough to suspect a freeze), and — the serious one — a Space-mash trained by
   dialogue-skipping can land on a freshly-opened question and silently submit an unread
   answer. For a game whose covenant is "the grading is fair," that's the crack to seal
   first. (Native prewarm merged tonight; web prewarm + universal input-arming + verdict
   parity are the fun-fixes stream.)
2. ⚙ **Feedback inequality between question kinds.** Parley and Binding Weave give a lingering
   "here's what you got wrong and why" card; the combat MCQ path just moves on. Every kind
   deserves Parley's standard — the teach-on-miss beat IS the retention product.
3. **The game undersells its own best moment.** Redemption — answering right what you once
   missed — is the reason the naive player would return, and the game currently marks it with
   *nothing*. The single highest-leverage fun addition available (§3.1).
4. **Sessions end with a shrug.** A run ends (victory, camp, retreat) and the game just…
   returns you. All the data for pride exists (facts kept, welds, streak, gold, what was
   reclaimed) and none of it is ceremonially shown.
5. **Sameness of challenge texture** pre-difficulty-tiers — designed, P1 merged, waiting on
   P2/P3; Beacon rarity should make big casts feel like events.

## 3. How it gets more fun (priority order, each concrete)

1. **Celebrate redemption: "Reclaimed from the Fade."** When a fact whose history contains a
   miss is answered q1, that's mechanically detectable in one line — give it a distinct beat:
   its own Clerk line ("The Fade held this one, once. It holds it no longer."), a lantern
   flare, and let it count as a Glow source alongside due-renewal (same §5.1 family, same
   plumbing). Cheap, aimed exactly at the documented would-return driver. The loop's peak
   deserves a bell.
2. **"The Night's Ledger" — a session-harvest screen.** On victory/camp/retreat: one warm
   page — facts kept, newly welded, reclaimed-from-the-Fade count, gold, streak. Ends every
   session on visible pride, gives quitting a ritual instead of a shrug, and doubles as the
   retention receipt the whole game exists to produce.
3. **Finish the fairness seal** (in flight): universal overlay arming, verdict-moment parity,
   web judge prewarm. Trust is the substrate every other fun sits on.
4. **Land difficulty tiers** (P2/P3): Beacon-hunting T3 casts turn typing effort into drama;
   the rider makes punching up legible. Already designed and half-shipped.
5. **Give the epic its surfaces.** «The First Lantern» + seven plates exist; a Records Office
   reading desk (one canto at a time, plate alongside) turns lore from files into a place —
   and the canto IV refrain is still the best epigraph candidate for the title screen.
6. **Let the map be a place.** The vault road's named nodes were a top-3 delight from a
   *screenshot* — lean in: names visible on hover everywhere, one line of flavor per node,
   same joke density.

## 4. What NOT to change

The failure writing's register (dry, wry, never scolding); teeth-but-fair damage numbers;
the deliberate silence of the vault-explore music contrast; the hamlet's unhurried pace; the
one-more-drill pull of the Wellspring's three-round shape. And no gamification-brain additions
(daily-quest guilt, login-streak pressure, currency confetti) — the game's dignity is part of
why the Clerk's respect feels earned.

## 5. Verdict

It's good — genuinely, not politely. The core loop is proven on a blind player; the voice is
distinctive; the structure (knowledge = moveset) is an actual idea, not a reskin. Tonight's
fun-killers are all sealable, two were sealed before this document was finished, and the two
additions that would most raise the ceiling (§3.1 redemption bell, §3.2 night's ledger) are
small, use data the game already has, and point directly at the moment players already love.
I would play it. I'd play it more tomorrow than today — and that's been true every day this
week, which is the healthiest thing you can say about a game in development.
