# The Fable of the Ledger Realms — Story Architecture

*Fable-authored (orchestrator, 2026-07-21). This document is the binding architecture for the world's
epic. The epic itself lives at `docs/story/the-first-lantern.md`. the author's directives, verbatim: "genuine
fantasy story", "don't make it about options or whatever", no knowledge-crate naming ("equity bay"
banned). The story must stand alone as fantasy even if every pack were deleted.*

---

## 1. The thesis (why this story, for this world)

Every mechanic in Fablewood is secretly about one thing: **renewal beats preservation**. A truth
tended returns stronger; a truth locked away dims, no matter how thick the vault. The epic exists to
make that law feel ancient and inevitable — so that when a player lights a brazier, renews a fading
fact, or breaks a Warden's shield with an attested truth, they are unknowingly re-enacting the
oldest story in the world.

The epic never names a game mechanic. It gives each one an ancestor.

## 2. Cosmology — the Three Givings

- **The Speaking.** The world was spoken, not built. Every true thing — river, name, bird, law of
  falling — is a *spoken line*, and the world holds together exactly as well as its lines are held.
  The sum of all held lines is called **the Ledger** — not a book of accounts but a covenant: *what
  is kept, keeps.* (In-world, "ledger" means this and only this. No commerce flavor, ever.)
- **The Fade.** Not a villain — the tide. The Speaking was one breath long, and ever since, the
  world has been slowly exhaling. The Fade is forgetting given weather: a grey unmaking that takes
  the unheld lines first — the name of a stream, then the stream. It cannot be defeated. It can only
  be *out-tended*.
- **The Glow.** When a held line is spoken again — truly spoken, from the keeper's own breath, not
  read off a page — it kindles. That kindling is Glow: the only light the Fade cannot dim, because
  it is not stored light but *living* light, the warmth of a truth in the act of being renewed.

## 3. The mythic ages (structure of the epic)

The epic is titled **«The First Lantern»** and runs in seven cantos. Prose, with one short verse
refrain closing each canto (these refrains are candidates for future leitmotif lyrics — composers:
Fable only, per policy).

1. **The Speaking** — creation; the world as a spoken thing; the first stillness before forgetting.
2. **The First Forgetting** — the Fade arrives as a season that does not end; the unheld lines go
   first; grief without an enemy to fight.
3. **The Lantern-Wright** — a nameless artisan discovers that a truth spoken *into* a wick holds its
   glow a while; forges the first lanterns; teaches that light must be *fed*, not kept. Dies with
   their own name unheld, which is why no one remembers it — the epic's first quiet wound.
4. **The Oath of the Wellspring** — the First Keeper kneels at the spring where all renewed things
   return, and swears the Keeper's Oath: *"What I tend returns. What returns, I tend."* The Oath is
   circular on purpose — it has no end condition. Keepers are those who accept a debt that is never
   paid off, only carried gladly. (Ancestor of: the daily return, the Hearth's unbroken nights.)
5. **The Sundering of the Warden** — the tragedy at the center. The Warden was the *best* of the
   early Keepers — the strongest memory, the deepest love for the lines. And out of love, the
   Warden's great heresy: if the Fade takes what is unheld, then seal what is held. The Warden built
   the Vaults — province upon province of locked truth — and shut the gates "until the weather
   turns." But a sealed line is an unheld line with better walls. Inside the vaults the lines dimmed
   into something neither kept nor taken: **the Dim** — wisps, wraiths, constructs; truths that
   forgot themselves but not their shape. The Warden, shield-bound at the last gate, now *demands
   proof* of any who enter: speak the line truly, or be turned away. The Warden is not a jailer of
   treasure but a mourner who cannot admit the funeral. (Ancestor of: vault dungeons, the Dim as
   enemies, gate trials, the Warden's shields broken only by attested truth.)
6. **The Long Dimming** — ages pass; the sealed provinces grey at their edges; hamlets huddle where
   Keepers still tend; the Ledger thins. The world forgets that it is forgetting — the Fade's
   cruelest trick. Lanterns become decoration. The Oath becomes a nursery rhyme.
7. **The Hamlet at the Wood's Heart** — Fablewood, now. A gatewarden who still locks the south gate
   out of habit older than he knows. A factor who appraises what others call worthless. A clerk who
   writes everything and explains nothing, in an office of records no one reads. A wellspring
   dismissed as a wishing-well. The canto ends on the epic's only prophecy, kept deliberately small:
   *"When the wick is trimmed and the gate is asked its question, a keeper comes — not the greatest,
   only the next."* (The player is "the next." Never more than that. Cozy, not chosen-one.)

## 4. Cast (mythic ↔ present-day mirrors)

| Mythic figure | Present mirror | Law they carry |
|---|---|---|
| The Lantern-Wright (name lost) | Berry (kindly, practical, underestimated) | Light must be fed, not kept |
| The First Keeper | The player-Keeper | The Oath is circular; the debt is carried gladly |
| The Warden | The Vault-Warden (boss) | Preservation without renewal is a slower Fade |
| The First Clerk, who wrote the Oath down and said nothing else | The Laconic Clerk | Record is not memory; the page serves the breath |
| The Gatewright who hung the first asked-question on a gate | Gatewarden Alder | A gate is a question, not a wall |
| The Factor of the First Hamlet, who priced things by how much light they could still hold | Mira the Factor | Worth is held light, not weight |

Present-day characters must NEVER be revealed as literally the mythic figures. Mirrors, descendants,
offices — the epic gains its warmth from recurrence, not immortality.

## 5. Register & style contract (binding on the epic's author)

- **Voice:** the shipped T3 register — hearth-warm, a little formal, never ornate past comfort.
  Sentences that could be read aloud at a fireside. Humor is allowed but dry and kind (the Clerk's
  line in canto 7 should earn a smile).
- **Verse refrains:** one per canto, 4–6 lines, simple meter, built to be sung. The refrain of
  canto 4 (the Oath) is the epic's spine and must be quotable: it will be the game's epigraph.
- **Canon vocabulary (use):** the Ledger, the Fade, the Glow, the Dim, Keeper, lantern, wick,
  brazier, hearth, wellspring, vault, province, grains (time), weld (a truth held so long it
  load-bears), attest/attested, tend/tending, the Speaking.
- **Banned:** any real-world domain vocabulary (finance, law, startups — nothing like "equity bay",
  "term coast", "valuation vale"); game-mechanic words as mechanics (streak, rank, pip, HP, save);
  irony about the setting; grimdark; named apocalypse countdowns. The Fade is weather, not a doom
  clock.
- **Scale:** epic ≈ 5,000–7,000 words total. Cantos 4 and 5 are the longest (the Oath and the
  Sundering carry the theme). Canto 7 is the shortest and gentlest — it must land like coming home.

|---|---|
| Daily renewal / due queue | The circular Oath; the debt carried gladly |
| Glow pips from renewing a due truth | Living light of a line spoken from the breath |
| Braziers → lanterns mid-battle | The Lantern-Wright's law: light must be fed |
| Welded truths (long-held) | Load-bearing lines; the oldest beams of the world |
| The Dim (wisps/wraiths/constructs) | Sealed truths that forgot themselves but not their shape |
| Vault-Warden's shields / attestation | The mourner's demand: speak the line truly |
| Wellspring drills → Trimmed Wick | Kneeling at the spring; tomorrow's lantern lights easier |
| The Fade closing in at 0 vigor | The weather taking the untended |
| Hamlet camp heal | The hearth: the one fire the Fade has never taken |
